## The spread on a ship

Onshore, the crews move along the pipe. Offshore, the pipe moves through the crews. On a lay vessel, joints pass through a line of stations along the deck, called the **firing line**: bevelling and line-up, several welding stations, NDT, and field joint coating. After each joint is complete the vessel moves ahead one joint length and the pipe slides off the stern toward the seabed.

Between the vessel and the seabed, the suspended pipe is held in shape by **tension**. Losing tension lets the pipe sag too sharply and buckle.

## Overbend and sagbend

In S-lay the suspended pipe has two critical regions:

- **Overbend:** where the pipe curves downward over the stinger. Bending strain is set by the stinger's radius. The pipe may be strained beyond yield in a controlled way.
- **Sagbend:** where the pipe curves back to horizontal near the seabed. Curvature here is controlled by tension: more tension gives a longer, gentler curve. The pipe is also under full external pressure here, so bending and collapse combine.

If tension is lost, for example by a tensioner failure or sudden vessel movement, the sagbend tightens and the pipe can buckle. A **wet buckle**, where the pipe also floods, suddenly multiplies its weight. **Buckle arrestors**, thick rings at intervals, limit how far a collapse can run.

## Welding and inspection offshore

Vessel time is expensive, so cycle time per joint drives everything:

- mechanised gas metal arc welding at several stations,
- automated ultrasonic testing with fracture-mechanics acceptance criteria, giving a result in about a minute,
- induction-heated field joint coating with fast-setting infill,
- welds qualified for installation strain, with tight limits on misalignment.

## Before and after the lay

**Before:**

- route survey of the seabed and removal or avoidance of obstacles,
- pre-lay trenching or seabed preparation where needed,
- crossings of existing pipelines and cables prepared with mattresses.

**During:**

- touchdown monitoring by remotely operated vehicle,
- **abandonment and recovery:** if weather worsens, the pipe end is sealed, lowered to the seabed on a cable and picked up later.

**After:**

- as-laid survey,
- span correction,
- **trenching** (jetting, ploughing or mechanical cutting) and backfill or rock placement,
- tie-ins to risers and subsea structures with spools,
- flooding, cleaning, gauging, hydrotest, dewatering and drying, as onshore.

<Callout type="note">
Water depth, pipe diameter and whether the pipe is concrete coated decide the method for most projects. Shallow water and large concrete-coated pipe point to S-lay. Small flowlines point to reel-lay. Very deep water with large pipe points to J-lay.
</Callout>

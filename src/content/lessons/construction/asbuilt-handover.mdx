## The pipeline you cannot see

When construction ends, the pipeline is buried. From then on, what the operator knows about it is what the records say. A pipeline with complete, traceable records can be assessed, re-rated, repaired and inspected efficiently for fifty years. One without them must be treated with suspicion, excavated to find out basic facts, or re-tested.

## Traceability

The core idea is an unbroken chain from a point on the ground to the documents that prove what is there:

**location → weld number → pipe joint number → heat number → mill test certificate**

and in parallel:

**weld number → welding procedure → welder → NDT report → repair record**

This chain is built stage by stage: at stringing (pipe to location), at welding (weld numbers), at NDT, at lowering-in (survey) and at tie-in. Assembled afterwards from memory, it is never complete.

## The as-built survey

The most important single record is where the pipe actually is.

- **Position and level of the top of pipe**, surveyed in the open trench before backfill, typically at every girth weld and at bends, crossings and features.
- **Weld numbers** attached to each surveyed point.
- **Final ground level** after reinstatement, giving depth of cover.
- **Features:** valves, tees, bends, anodes, test posts, casings, crossings of other services.

This survey gives every later in-line inspection a map to be matched against, and every future excavator an accurate location.

## Commissioning

Commissioning brings the completed pipeline into service in controlled steps:

1. **Mechanical completion.** Everything is built, tested and inspected. Remaining items are listed on a **punch list**, graded by whether they must be closed before start-up.
2. **Pre-commissioning.** Cleaning, drying, inerting, valve and instrument function checks, CP energised, SCADA points verified end to end.
3. **Pre-startup safety review.** A formal check that the system, procedures and people are ready.
4. **Introduction of product.** For gas, air is displaced with nitrogen and then gas, so that gas and air never mix. For liquids, the line is filled behind pigs to control the interface.
5. **Pressurisation** in stages to operating pressure, with leak checks at flanges and valves.
6. **Performance run** and **handover** to operations.

## Lessons from the field

- Records are compiled **as work proceeds**, by people assigned to do so, and checked weekly.
- Each record is tied to a **location**, not just a date.
- Digital data capture in the field (tablets, barcodes on pipe) avoids transcription errors.
- The handover package is reviewed by the **operations team** before acceptance.

<Callout type="note">
Several regulators now require pipeline records to be **traceable, verifiable and complete**. Where an operator cannot show the material grade or test pressure of a segment from reliable records, the allowable operating pressure may have to be reduced until it is re-established by testing.
</Callout>

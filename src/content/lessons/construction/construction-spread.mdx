## A factory that moves

In a factory the product moves past the workers. On a pipeline the product stays where it is and the factory moves. Each crew does one job, then advances along the right of way, followed by the next crew. The whole train of crews and equipment is the **spread**. On a large project it stretches over tens of kilometres and employs hundreds of people.

A long pipeline is divided into several spreads working at once, each building 50 to 150 km.

## How the spread is sequenced

- **Linear work:** survey, clearing, stringing, welding, coating, trenching, lowering-in and backfill follow each other continuously. Their speed sets the daily progress, typically 0.5 to 3 km per day depending on diameter, terrain and welding method.
- **The pacing crew** is usually mainline welding. Everything else is sized to keep up with it and not get in its way.
- **Special sections** are built separately by dedicated crews: road and wadi crossings, valve stations, scraper traps, steep slopes and rock. The main spread skips them and moves on.
- **Tie-in crews** follow behind, joining the strings to each other and to the special sections.
- **Testing and reinstatement** crews work in completed test sections.

Whether trenching comes before or after welding depends on the ground. In stable soil the trench is often dug first. In sand that collapses, or where open trench is a hazard, it is dug just ahead of lowering-in, as shown above.

## What controls progress

- **Terrain:** rock, sabkha and dunes slow trenching and access.
- **Welding rate and repair rate.**
- **Weather:** wind and sand stop welding and coating; rain floods trenches; summer heat limits working hours.
- **Material supply:** pipe, bends and coating materials must arrive in the right sequence.
- **Permits and crossings:** each one can hold the spread.
- **Inspection hold points.**

## Quality control and hold points

Construction quality is managed through an **inspection and test plan (ITP)**. For every activity it lists what is checked, against which standard, by whom, and whether work must stop until the check is accepted:

- **Hold point:** work cannot proceed until the inspector signs.
- **Witness point:** the inspector is notified and may attend.
- **Review:** records are checked afterward.

Typical hold points are welding procedure qualification, pre-lowering holiday test, trench bottom acceptance before lowering-in, pre-backfill survey, and the hydrotest. Anything that becomes invisible after the next stage needs to be inspected before it.

<Callout type="field">
The single most valuable habit on a spread is to inspect work **before it is covered**. A coating defect found before lowering-in takes ten minutes to repair. The same defect found by a survey five years later needs an excavation.
</Callout>

## Safety on the spread

Pipeline construction combines heavy lifting, deep excavation, moving plant, radiation, high pressure and remote locations. The main controls are permits to work, lifting plans for sidebooms, excavation rules, radiography exclusion zones, traffic management on the right of way, heat stress management and emergency response for remote sites.

## What a tie-in is

The main spread welds long strings beside the trench and lowers them in. Wherever a string ends, there is a gap: at a road or wadi crossing built separately, at a valve station, at the start of a new day's string, or at a hydrotest section boundary. The weld that closes each gap is a **tie-in**.

A tie-in differs from a mainline weld in every way that matters:

- It is made **in the trench**, in a bell hole, often in poor light and awkward positions.
- The two pipe ends are **already restrained** by backfill, so they cannot be moved freely to get good fit-up.
- An **external** clamp must be used, since an internal clamp could not be retrieved.
- The last tie-ins in a test section, called **golden welds**, are made after the hydrotest and so are never pressure tested.

For these reasons tie-in welds have the highest repair rates and the tightest controls.

## Controls on tie-in welds

- **No forcing.** The ends are cut to fit so they meet in line without jacking or pulling. Forced alignment locks in stress that stays for the life of the pipeline.
- **Enough free pipe.** A sufficient length is left uncovered each side so the ends can be aligned elastically.
- **Low-hydrogen, vertical-up procedures**, with a separately qualified tie-in procedure and welders.
- **Extended preheat and delay before inspection**, because restraint increases the risk of delayed hydrogen cracking. NDT is often carried out 24 hours or more after welding.
- **100 % inspection by two methods** for golden welds, commonly radiography plus ultrasonic or magnetic particle testing.
- **Safe excavation.** The bell hole is sloped or shored and has clear exits.

## Tie-in temperature

A buried pipeline is "locked in" at the temperature of its final tie-ins. Every degree between that temperature and the operating temperature becomes stress (module M2).

- On **hot lines**, tie-ins are made when the pipe is warm, typically in the afternoon, and the specification states a **minimum tie-in temperature**. A weld made at 15 °C on a winter night instead of 40 °C adds about 60 MPa of compression in service.
- The pipe temperature at each tie-in is measured and recorded on the weld record and as-built data.
- Long strings left open in the sun move by tens of millimetres between day and night. Tie-ins are planned for a stable period.

<Callout type="field">
If two pipe ends that fitted at noon have a 40 mm gap at dawn, the pipe is telling you how much it moves. Do not close that gap with a sideboom and weld it. Wait for the temperature at which the tie-in was planned.
</Callout>

## Cleaning, gauging and testing

Before a section is tested, pigs are run through it with compressed air or water:

1. **Cleaning pigs** with brushes and scrapers remove mill scale, sand, welding debris and anything left inside.
2. A **gauging pig** carries an aluminium plate, usually 95 % of the minimum internal diameter. If the plate arrives bent or notched, something is reducing the bore: a dent, a buckle, excessive weld penetration or an ovalised bend. It must be found and corrected before testing.
3. Increasingly, a **caliper (geometry) pig** is run instead or as well, which measures and locates every dent and ovality.

The hydrostatic test itself is the subject of module M10.

## Reinstatement

The right of way is returned as close to its original condition as practical.

- **Clean up.** All skids, welding rod stubs, coating waste, drums and scrap removed. Nothing is buried in the trench.
- **Regrade** to original contours, with a crown over the trench.
- **Topsoil** replaced where it was stripped, and compaction from construction traffic relieved in farmland.
- **Drainage** restored: wadi beds and banks re-profiled and protected, culverts and ditches reopened.
- **Erosion control:** slope breakers, mats, rock or vegetation on slopes; surface stabilisation over the trench in windblown sand.
- **Roads, fences, walls and irrigation** repaired to at least their previous standard.
- **Permanent markers,** test posts and fences at above-ground facilities installed.
- **Sign-off** by landowners and authorities, compared with the pre-construction record.

Reinstatement is inspected again after the first rains or the first season, when trench settlement and erosion show up.

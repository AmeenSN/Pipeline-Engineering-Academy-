## The girth weld

A 100 km pipeline in 12 m joints has more than 8000 girth welds. Each is made outdoors, in position, around a fixed horizontal pipe, by a team working against the clock. Welding is where pipeline construction quality is mostly won or lost.

## Building up the weld

<WeldJoint client:visible />

Step the "passes completed" slider from 0 to 4 to build the weld. Then change the voltage, current and travel speed to see how heat input responds.

On a mainline spread the work is split:

- The **front end** aligns each new joint with an internal line-up clamp and makes the root and hot pass. As soon as the hot pass is complete the clamp is released and the front end moves to the next joint.
- The **back end** follows, with several welding stations each adding fill passes and the cap.

## What must be controlled

- **Fit-up.** Root gap and high-low (the radial offset between the two pipe ends). Poor fit-up is the root cause of most root defects.
- **Preheat.** The joint is heated, typically to 50 to 150 °C depending on grade, wall and process, to slow cooling and let hydrogen escape. Checked with contact thermometers or temperature crayons.
- **Interpass temperature.** Kept within limits between passes.
- **Heat input.** Controlled through current, voltage and travel speed.
- **Hydrogen.** Low-hydrogen electrodes kept dry in heated quivers; time between root and hot pass limited; no welding on wet pipe.
- **Weather.** Wind shelters for gas-shielded processes; no welding in rain or sandstorm.
- **Cleanliness.** Slag removed and each pass ground or brushed before the next.
- **No arc strikes** outside the weld groove. An arc strike leaves a small, hard, crack-prone spot on the pipe.

## Qualification

**Welding procedure specification (WPS).** A written recipe: joint design, process, consumables, preheat, electrical ranges, number of passes. It is proven by welding a test joint and destructively testing it (tensile, bend, nick-break, and for most projects Charpy and hardness). The record of that test is the procedure qualification record (PQR).

**Essential variables.** If any of a defined list changes beyond its limit, for example pipe grade, wall thickness range, process or consumable, the procedure must be requalified.

**Welder qualification.** Each welder welds a test joint to the procedure, which is inspected and tested. Qualification is specific to process, position and diameter and thickness range.

## Inspection

Every weld is visually inspected. Then, on most pipelines, 100 % are examined by a volumetric method:

| | Radiography (RT) | Automated ultrasonics (AUT) |
|---|---|---|
| How | X-ray or gamma source inside the pipe exposes film or a detector wrapped outside | Phased-array and time-of-flight probes scan round the weld on a band |
| Finds best | Volumetric defects: porosity, slag, burn-through | Planar defects: lack of fusion, cracks |
| Sizing | Length only | Length and height, enabling fracture-mechanics acceptance |
| Speed | Minutes per weld plus processing | Result in about a minute |
| Safety | Radiation exclusion zone | No radiation |
| Suits | Manual welding, small projects | Mechanised welding, large projects |

**Acceptance criteria.** API 1104 gives workmanship limits on defect type and length. Its Annex A allows wider limits based on an engineering critical assessment, where defect height is known and weld toughness is proven.

**Repairs.** A rejected weld is repaired by grinding out the defect and re-welding to a qualified repair procedure, then re-inspected. Cracks usually mean the weld is cut out. The **repair rate** (a few percent is typical) is tracked by welder and by cause.

<Callout type="field">
Lack of fusion on the bevel wall lies almost parallel to an X-ray beam and can be invisible on a radiograph, yet it is the most common defect in mechanised narrow-gap welds. That is why mechanised welding and AUT are used together.
</Callout>

<Worked title="Heat input of a fill pass">

**Given:** 26 V, 160 A, travel speed 300 mm/min.

$$
HI = \frac{26 \times 160 \times 60}{1000 \times 300} = 0.83 \text{ kJ/mm}
$$

If the welder slows to 150 mm/min, heat input doubles to 1.66 kJ/mm. If the procedure was qualified for 0.6 to 1.2 kJ/mm, the slower pass is outside the procedure, even though nothing else changed.

## What a coating must do

A coating works by keeping water, oxygen and ions away from the steel, and by being an electrical insulator so that cathodic protection current is needed only at defects. To do that for fifty years it needs:

- **Adhesion** to steel that survives heat and moisture.
- **Low permeability** to water and oxygen.
- **High electrical resistance.**
- **Resistance to cathodic disbondment:** the alkaline conditions CP creates at a holiday must not lift the coating around it.
- **Mechanical toughness:** impact, abrasion, indentation and bending during handling, field bending and backfill.
- **Temperature stability** at the operating temperature.
- **Resistance to soil stress,** chemicals, bacteria and, during storage, sunlight.
- **Failure that does not shield.** If it disbonds, CP current should still be able to reach the steel.

## How coating is applied

Mainline coating is applied in a plant, where conditions can be controlled:

1. **Pre-heat and dry** the pipe.
2. **Abrasive blast** to near-white metal with an angular profile of roughly 50 to 100 microns. This is the most important step: adhesion depends on it.
3. **Inspect and clean** the surface; remove dust; check for soluble salts, with a phosphoric or chromate wash where specified.
4. **Heat** the pipe to about 200 to 240 °C by induction.
5. **Spray FBE powder** electrostatically. It melts, flows and cures on the hot steel.
6. For three-layer systems, **extrude adhesive and polyolefin** over the still-reactive epoxy, by side-wrap or crosshead die.
7. **Quench** with water.
8. **Inspect:** 100 % holiday test, thickness, and visual.
9. **Cut back** the coating at the ends for welding.

## Quality tests

- **Holiday detection** on every pipe.
- **Thickness** of each layer.
- **Adhesion:** peel test for three-layer systems; knife or pull-off for FBE.
- **Cathodic disbondment** test: a hole is made in the coating and the sample is held under CP at elevated temperature. The radius of disbonding must be small.
- **Impact** and **indentation** resistance.
- **Flexibility:** a strip is bent, simulating field bending.
- **Hot-water soak** adhesion and **degree of cure** (thermal analysis) for FBE.

## Field joints, bends and repairs

The coating system is only as good as its field joints (module M8). Selection rule: the joint coating must be **compatible** with the mainline coating and rated for the same temperature.

| Mainline coating | Usual field joint coating |
|---|---|
| 3LPE | Heat-shrink sleeve with epoxy primer |
| 3LPP | Polypropylene sleeve, flame-sprayed or injection-moulded PP |
| FBE | FBE applied by field machine, or liquid epoxy |
| Any, on bends and fittings | Liquid epoxy or polyurethane, or FBE in a plant |

Damage found by holiday testing is repaired with melt sticks, patches or liquid epoxy to an approved procedure, then retested.

## Selecting a coating

Consider, in order:

1. **Operating temperature** including upsets.
2. **Terrain and installation method:** rock, HDD and offshore need toughness.
3. **Soil corrosivity and water table.**
4. **Field bending** requirements.
5. **Compatibility with CP** and with concrete coating if used.
6. **Availability** of qualified applicators near the project.
7. **Field joint system** that completes the package.

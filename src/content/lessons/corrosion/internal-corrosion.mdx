## No water, no corrosion

Dry gas and stabilised, dehydrated crude do not corrode steel. Internal corrosion needs **free water** in contact with the pipe wall, carrying a corrosive agent:

- **CO₂**, forming carbonic acid,
- **H₂S**, corrosive and a cause of cracking,
- **Oxygen**, mainly in water injection and seawater lines,
- **Bacteria**, particularly sulphate-reducers under deposits,
- **Organic acids and chlorides** that make everything worse.

Module M1 showed how to estimate the CO₂ rate and decide between carbon steel and resistant alloy. This lesson covers how a carbon steel line is kept alive.

## Where it happens

Internal corrosion is not uniform. It concentrates where water or solids collect:

- **The bottom of the pipe** (6 o'clock position) in stratified flow.
- **Low points** and the upward-sloping sections after them, where water pools.
- **Dead legs:** branches, drains and bypasses with no flow.
- **Under deposits** of sand, wax, scale or corrosion product, which shield the wall from inhibitor and shelter bacteria.
- **The top of the pipe** in wet gas lines, where water condenses on the cooler upper wall and inhibitor carried in the liquid cannot reach it.
- **Welds,** where the root profile disturbs flow.

Velocity matters both ways. Too slow and water and solids settle. Too fast and the flow strips inhibitor films and erodes the wall.

## Monitoring

Control only works if you know it is working. Methods are combined, because each sees something different.

| Method | What it measures | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weight-loss coupons | Average metal loss over weeks or months | Simple and reliable; slow |
| Electrical resistance (ER) probes | Cumulative metal loss, continuously | Works in any fluid |
| Linear polarisation (LPR) probes | Instantaneous rate | Needs a continuous water phase |
| Fluid sampling | Water content, iron, residual inhibitor, bacteria | Indirect but frequent |
| Pig debris analysis | Solids, corrosion product, bacteria | Shows what is on the wall |
| Ultrasonic wall thickness | Actual wall at fixed points | Direct but local |
| In-line inspection | Wall loss over the whole pipeline | The definitive check, every few years |

Coupons and probes must be placed where corrosion is expected: at the bottom of the pipe and at low points, not wherever access is easiest.

<Callout type="field">
A well-designed inhibition system fails in mundane ways: an empty chemical tank, a blocked injection quill, a pump left off after maintenance. Tracking inhibitor availability as a key performance indicator finds these before the wall does.
</Callout>

## Design for corrosion control

Decisions at the design stage make operation easier:

- Make the line **piggable**, with traps sized for inspection tools.
- Avoid **dead legs** and unnecessary low points.
- Provide **injection points, sampling points and probe fittings** at the inlet and at the locations where water is predicted to collect.
- Choose a **velocity** range that keeps solids moving without stripping films.
- Specify sour-service or HIC-resistant pipe where H₂S is present.

<Callout type="aramco">
Saudi Aramco's SAES-L-133 defines when internal corrosion protection is required for pipelines and which methods apply by service, and SAES-L-420 requires scraper traps so that lines can be cleaned and inspected. Sour, wet crude and gas systems are the main internal corrosion concern.
</Callout>

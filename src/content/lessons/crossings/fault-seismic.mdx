## Two kinds of earthquake loading

**Wave propagation.** Seismic waves travel through the ground and the buried pipe moves with the soil. The strains induced are small. Modern welded steel pipelines in good condition almost never fail from shaking alone.

**Permanent ground deformation (PGD).** The ground itself ends up in a different place: a fault ruptures, a slope slides, or loose saturated sand liquefies and spreads. The pipe is forced to follow, and the movement can be metres. This is what breaks pipelines.

Much of the Arabian plate interior has low seismicity, but pipelines near plate boundaries (the Red Sea and Gulf of Aqaba margin, the Zagros belt) and slope and subsidence hazards anywhere need this assessment.

## Strain-based design

Ordinary pipeline design limits **stress** to a fraction of yield. That cannot work for a fault crossing: no practical pipe stays below yield when the ground moves a metre or more.

Instead, the pipe is allowed to yield and the design limits **strain**:

- **Tensile strain limit:** set by the girth welds, typically 1 to 3 % with qualified, overmatched welds and tight control of flaws.
- **Compressive strain limit:** set by the onset of local buckling (wrinkling), which depends strongly on D/t. Typically 0.5 to 1.5 %.

The analysis is a non-linear finite-element model of the pipe, with the soil represented by springs in the axial, lateral, upward and downward directions.

## What the designer can do

- **Crossing angle.** Choose the angle that puts the pipe in tension.
- **Straight pipe, no bends** for a few hundred metres each side, so movement is absorbed over a long length instead of concentrating at a bend.
- **Heavier wall, lower D/t** to delay wrinkling, in steel with good strain-hardening.
- **Reduce soil restraint.** Shallow cover, a wide trench with sloped sides, and loose, uniform granular backfill let the pipe slide and shed load. Some crossings wrap the pipe in geotextile or foam.
- **Go above ground.** On sliding supports, the pipe can move freely. The Trans-Alaska pipeline crosses the Denali fault this way, and survived a 5.5 m offset in 2002.
- **High-quality welds.** Strain capacity depends on weld strength overmatch, toughness and small allowable flaw sizes.
- **Isolation valves** each side of an active fault.
- **Monitoring.** Strain gauges, fibre-optic sensing, survey monuments and inertial in-line inspection to detect movement.

<Callout type="note">
The same measures that help at a fault (loose backfill, shallow cover) are the opposite of what resists upheaval buckling. On a hot line crossing a fault, the two requirements must be balanced by analysis.
</Callout>

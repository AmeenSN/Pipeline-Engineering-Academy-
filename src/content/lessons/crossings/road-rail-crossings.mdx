## What is special about a crossing

At a crossing the pipeline:

- carries **repeated wheel or axle loads** on top of soil load and internal pressure,
- lies where excavation for repair is **difficult and disruptive**,
- must usually be installed **without closing** the road or railway,
- sits in land controlled by **another authority**, which sets its own conditions.

For these reasons the crossing is designed separately, has its own drawing, and normally uses heavier-wall pipe than the line on either side.

## Requirements at a crossing

| Item | Typical requirement |
|---|---|
| Crossing angle | As near 90° as practical, not less than about 30° |
| Cover under a road surface | 1.2 m minimum |
| Cover under railway track (uncased) | 1.8 m minimum |
| Cover under roadside ditches | 0.9 m minimum |
| Wall thickness | Heavier wall or lower design factor through the crossing |
| Extent of heavy wall | Across the full road reserve plus a margin each side |
| Girth welds | 100 % inspected; as few as possible under the road |
| Bends | None under the road. Vertical bends start outside the reserve |
| Coating | Abrasion-resistant overcoat for bored pipe |
| Markers | A pipeline marker each side of the crossing |

ASME B31.8 lowers the design factor at many crossings (for example to 0.60 in Class 1 for an uncased road crossing), which increases the wall directly.

## Cased or uncased

A **casing** is a larger steel pipe installed under the road with the carrier pipe threaded through it on insulating spacers. The ends are sealed and vent pipes rise to the surface.

Casings were once standard, on the idea that they carry the traffic load and let the carrier be withdrawn. Experience has shown real drawbacks:

- The casing **shields** the carrier from cathodic protection current.
- If the carrier touches the casing (a **shorted casing**), CP current is drained and the annulus can corrode the carrier unseen.
- Water enters through failed end seals.
- The carrier in a casing cannot be inspected by above-ground coating surveys.

Modern practice, reflected in API RP 1102, is an **uncased** crossing with thicker-wall carrier pipe designed to take the loads directly. Casings are used only where the road or rail authority demands one, or where the ground makes boring the coated carrier impractical.

## The API RP 1102 design method

API RP 1102 gives a step-by-step check for uncased steel pipelines:

1. Start with the wall thickness from pressure design, at the crossing's design factor.
2. Calculate the **circumferential stress from earth load**.
3. Calculate the **cyclic circumferential and longitudinal stresses from live load** (wheel or axle load with an impact factor), from design curves that depend on depth, diameter, wall thickness and soil.
4. Add the **internal pressure** stresses.
5. Check the **total effective stress** against the allowable (SMYS × design factor).
6. Check **fatigue** of the girth weld and of the longitudinal seam against endurance limits, since traffic applies millions of cycles.

If any check fails, increase wall thickness or depth. The explorer above uses a simpler screening estimate, and the real method must be followed for design.

## Building the crossing

- **Open cut.** The road is trenched, half at a time or with a diversion. Cheap and simple. Used for tracks and minor roads. Backfill must be compacted in layers and the pavement reinstated to the authority's standard.
- **Auger boring (thrust boring).** A boring machine in a launch pit drives an auger inside the pipe or casing, pushing it forward under the road to a reception pit. The usual method for paved roads and railways up to about 100 m.
- **Pipe jacking, microtunnelling and HDD** for longer or more difficult crossings (next lesson).

<Callout type="field">
On a bored crossing the carrier pipe coating is dragged through the ground. Specify an abrasion-resistant overcoat, and check the coating by electrical test after installation, before the tie-in welds are made.

<Worked title="Wall thickness through a highway crossing">

**Given:** NPS 24 gas line, P = 9.6 MPa, X65, Class 1. Line pipe wall from the M2 lesson is 9.5 mm at F = 0.72 (9.04 mm required).

**At the crossing:** ASME B31.8 requires F = 0.60 for an uncased crossing of a hard-surfaced road in Class 1.

$$
t = \frac{9.6 \times 610}{2 \times 450 \times 0.60} = 10.84 \text{ mm} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{select } 11.1 \text{ mm or } 12.7 \text{ mm}
$$

**Choose 12.7 mm.** The extra wall also helps the API RP 1102 live-load and fatigue checks and matches a wall already used elsewhere on the line.

**Extent:** the heavy wall runs across the road reserve and a margin beyond each boundary, with transition welds made in the open trench where they can be inspected.

## Why go trenchless

Open-cut trenching is the cheapest way to install pipe, until the surface cannot be disturbed. Trenchless methods install the pipe from pits or surface entry points with nothing in between touched. They are used for busy roads and railways, rivers and canals, wetlands, congested utility corridors and environmentally sensitive ground.

## Designing the profile

An HDD profile is a straight **entry tangent**, a downward **curve**, a straight section at depth, an upward curve and an **exit tangent**.

- **Entry angle:** usually 8° to 20°, set by the rig.
- **Exit angle:** usually 5° to 12°, kept low so the pipe string can be lifted into the hole without over-bending.
- **Bend radius:** the pipe is pulled round the curves elastically, so the radius must respect the elastic bend limit. A common rule for steel pipe is

$$
R \geq 1200 \times D
$$

  that is, 100 feet of radius per inch of diameter.
- **Depth:** enough cover beneath the obstacle to contain the drilling mud pressure, typically at least 6 to 10 m under a river bed, and below the scour level.

The curves need horizontal space. A deep crossing with a large pipe is therefore necessarily long.

## Stresses on the pipe

The pipe in an HDD sees loads an ordinary buried line does not:

- **Pull force** during pullback: friction on the rollers and in the hole, plus drag from the mud.
- **Bending** round the curves, during installation and permanently.
- **External pressure** from the drilling mud on an empty pipe, which can govern wall thickness (collapse, module M2).
- **Combined** tension, bending and external pressure at the same time.

Heavy-wall pipe and an abrasion-resistant coating over the main coating are standard.

## What can go wrong

- **Frac-out:** drilling mud breaks through to the surface or river bed when its pressure exceeds what the cover can contain.
- **Hole collapse** in loose sand or gravel.
- **Stuck pipe** during pullback, if the hole is not clean or the pull is interrupted.
- **Steering error**, hitting another utility or exiting off target.
- **Coating damage**, invisible once the pipe is in the ground.

A thorough geotechnical investigation along the bore line is the best protection against all of these.

<Callout type="field">
Pullback is done in one continuous operation, day and night if necessary. Stopping allows the mud to gel and the hole to close in on the pipe. The whole string is therefore welded, inspected, coated and hydrotested before pullback begins.
</Callout>

<Worked title="Minimum length of an HDD river crossing">

**Given:** NPS 24 pipe (0.61 m), 18 m depth below entry level, 10° entry, 8° exit.

**Radius:** R = 1200 × 0.61 = 732 m.

**Entry side:** the curve from 10° to level drops R(1 − cos 10°) = 11.1 m over a run of R sin 10° = 127 m. The remaining 6.9 m of depth comes from the straight tangent: 6.9 / tan 10° = 39 m. Total 166 m.

**Exit side:** the curve drops R(1 − cos 8°) = 7.1 m over 102 m. Remaining 10.9 m from the tangent: 10.9 / tan 8° = 77 m. Total 179 m.

**Minimum crossing length:** 166 + 179 = **345 m**, with no straight section at depth. For a river 200 m wide needing full depth across its bed, the crossing is about 550 to 700 m.

**Bending stress in the curves:** E·D / 2R = 207 000 × 0.61 / (2 × 732) = 86 MPa, comfortably elastic.

</Worked>

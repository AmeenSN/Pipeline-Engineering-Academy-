## The hazard

In arid regions, rain is rare but intense. Runoff from a large rocky catchment concentrates in the wadi within hours, producing a short, fast, sediment-laden flood. Three things then happen to a pipeline crossing:

1. **Scour.** The flood lifts the bed material into suspension. The bed level during the peak can be metres below the dry bed. When the flood recedes the bed refills, so the evidence disappears.
2. **Exposure and spanning.** If scour reaches the pipe, the pipe is left unsupported in fast water, loaded by drag, debris impact and vortex-induced vibration (module M2).
3. **Bank erosion and channel migration.** The wadi shifts sideways, attacking the sloping sections of pipe at the banks where cover is least.

Perennial rivers, canals and tidal channels pose the same threats more continuously, with buoyancy and navigation (anchors, dredging) added.

## Estimating scour

The design scour depth comes from a hydrological study (module M3). For sandy beds a widely used first estimate is Lacey's regime formula:

$$
R = 0.473 \left( \frac{Q}{f} \right)^{1/3} \qquad f = 1.76 \sqrt{d_{50}}
$$

- $R$ is the normal scour depth in metres, measured **below the flood water level**.
- $Q$ is the design flood discharge in m³/s.
- $f$ is the silt factor, from the median grain size $d_{50}$ in millimetres.

The maximum scour is larger than $R$ where the flow is concentrated: about 1.27 R on a straight reach, 1.5 R at a moderate bend and 1.75 R or more at a sharp bend.

## Setting the burial depth

$$
\text{cover below bed} \geq \text{scour depth below bed} + \text{margin}
$$

The margin, commonly 1 to 2 m, covers the uncertainty in the flood estimate and in the scour formula. The deep section extends beyond the present banks to allow for channel migration, and the pipe rises to normal cover with **sag bends well outside the banks**.

Design floods for pipeline crossings are typically the 1-in-50-year to 1-in-100-year event.

## Keeping the pipe stable

Even with good burial, crossings are designed to survive exposure:

- **Concrete weight coating** gives negative buoyancy and mechanical protection against debris.
- **Heavier wall pipe** through the crossing.
- **No girth weld defects:** 100 % inspection.
- **Selected backfill**, with the bed restored to its natural level so the crossing does not create a hump or hollow that triggers local scour.
- **Bank protection:** rock riprap, gabion mattresses or articulated concrete mats on the banks, keyed in at the toe.

## Crossing methods

| Method | When used |
|---|---|
| Open cut in the dry | Wadis and seasonal channels: the usual method. Build outside the flood season. |
| Open cut in flowing water | Small perennial streams, with flumes or dam-and-pump to divert flow. |
| HDD or microtunnel | Major rivers, navigable channels, environmentally sensitive water, or where the scour depth makes open cut impractical. |
| Aerial crossing (pipe bridge) | Deep gorges and canals where burial is not feasible. Exposed to damage and needs expansion design. |

## Inspection in service

Because the bed refills after a flood, a crossing can lose and regain its cover without anyone seeing. Operators therefore:

- patrol crossings after every significant flood,
- survey depth of cover at intervals,
- look for exposed pipe, eroded banks and damaged protection works,
- review in-line inspection data for dents or strain at crossings.

<Worked title="Burial depth at a wadi crossing">

**Given:** design flood 400 m³/s, medium sand bed with d50 = 0.5 mm, flood depth 2 m, straight reach, margin 1.5 m.

**Silt factor:** f = 1.76 × √0.5 = 1.24.

**Normal scour below flood level:**

$$
R = 0.473 \times \left( \frac{400}{1.24} \right)^{1/3} = 0.473 \times 6.86 = 3.2 \text{ m}
$$

**Maximum scour on a straight reach:** 1.27 × 3.2 = 4.1 m below flood level.

**Below the bed:** 4.1 − 2.0 = 2.1 m.

**Required cover:** 2.1 + 1.5 = **3.6 m** below the wadi bed.

At a sharp bend the same flood would scour 1.75 × 3.2 − 2.0 = 3.7 m below bed, needing 5.2 m of cover: a strong argument for moving the crossing to a straight reach.

</Worked>

## What a pipeline is for

A pipeline moves fluid from where it is produced to where it is processed or used. Per tonne moved it is the safest, cheapest and lowest-emission method of land transport, which is why the world has several million kilometres of them.

A pipeline engineer's job is to make sure that the pipe **contains** the fluid for decades, at the lowest life-cycle cost, without harming people or the environment.

## Facilities along the line

A pipeline is more than pipe. Along a typical transmission line you find:

- **Pump stations** (liquids) or **compressor stations** (gas) that add the energy lost to friction.
- **Scraper traps** (pig launchers and receivers) at each end and at intermediate stations.
- **Sectionalizing (block) valve stations** that divide the line so a section can be isolated.
- **Metering stations** where custody of the product changes hands.
- **Pressure reduction and relief** stations that protect lower-rated systems downstream.
- **Cathodic protection stations** with rectifiers and ground beds.
- **SCADA and telecom** sites, usually sharing valve stations, often linked by a fibre-optic cable laid in the same trench.

## Onshore buried, above ground, or offshore

- **Buried onshore** pipelines are the norm. The soil protects the pipe from damage, sun and temperature swings, and restrains it.
- **Above-ground** pipelines rest on supports. They are easier to inspect and avoid upheaval buckling, but they are exposed and must be allowed to expand.
- **Offshore** pipelines lie on or under the seabed, and are governed by installation loads, stability under waves and currents, and external pressure.

This academy focuses on buried onshore pipelines, with module M12 covering the other two.

## The life cycle of a pipeline

1. **Concept and feasibility.** Flow rate, route options, diameter and pressure studies.
2. **Front-end engineering (FEED).** Route survey, hydraulic design, wall thickness, material selection, cost estimate.
3. **Detailed design.** Alignment sheets, crossing drawings, stress analysis, specifications.
4. **Procurement.** Line pipe, valves, bends and coating, often the longest lead items.
5. **Construction.** Right of way, welding, lowering-in, backfill, tie-ins.
6. **Testing and commissioning.** Hydrotest, drying, product fill.
7. **Operation and integrity management.** Monitoring, pigging, inspection, repair.
8. **Decommissioning.** Cleaning and removal or abandonment in place.

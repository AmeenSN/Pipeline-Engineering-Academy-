## Components and the weakest link

Line pipe is designed by calculation. Flanges, fittings and valves are mostly **rated**: they are made to dimensional standards that assign each a pressure rating. The maximum allowable operating pressure (MAOP) of a pipeline system is the lowest of the pipe design pressure, the test pressure divided by the test factor, and the rating of the weakest component.

## Flange classes

ASME B16.5 groups flanges into pressure **classes**: 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500 and 2500. The class number is a label, not a pressure. The actual rating depends on the flange material and falls as temperature rises.

For ordinary carbon steel flanges (material group 1.1) at ambient temperature:

| Class | Rating at 38 °C | Typical pipeline use |
|---|---|---|
| 150 | 19.6 bar (285 psi) | Water, low-pressure gathering |
| 300 | 51.1 bar (740 psi) | Gathering and distribution |
| 600 | 102.1 bar (1480 psi) | Most transmission pipelines |
| 900 | 153.2 bar (2220 psi) | High-pressure gas, injection |
| 1500 | 255.3 bar (3705 psi) | Wellhead flowlines, injection |
| 2500 | 425.5 bar (6170 psi) | Very high pressure |

Try this: set 96 bar at 60 °C and read off Class 600. Now raise the temperature toward 100 °C: the Class 600 rating drops below the design pressure and the selector jumps to Class 900. Temperature derating catches many designers out.

<Callout type="note">
Many transmission pipelines are designed to exactly the Class 600 ambient rating: 102 bar, or 1480 psi. The pipe could usually take more, but going higher would push every valve and flange up a class.
</Callout>

### Flange details that matter

- **Size range.** B16.5 covers up to NPS 24. Larger flanges are to ASME B16.47 (Series A or B) or MSS SP-44.
- **Type.** Weld-neck flanges are standard for pipelines: the tapered hub transfers stress smoothly and the butt weld can be radiographed.
- **Face.** Raised face (RF) with a spiral-wound gasket up to about Class 600 to 900; ring-type joint (RTJ) with a metal ring for higher classes and sour service.
- **Bore.** The flange bore must match the pipe wall, or the mismatch must be tapered.
- **Material strength.** A standard flange forging is weaker than X65 pipe. High-yield flanges (for example ASTM A694 F65) are used so that the hub can match the pipe wall.

Every flange is a potential leak and a maintenance item. Buried pipelines use as few as possible: valves are often welded-end.

## Fittings

**Butt-weld fittings** (ASME B16.9, and MSS SP-75 for high-strength grades) include elbows, tees, reducers and caps. They are designed to be at least as strong as the matching pipe.

Fittings that need special thought on a pipeline:

- **Elbows.** Standard long-radius elbows have a radius of 1.5 D, too tight for inspection pigs. Mainline changes of direction use bends of 3 D or 5 D and larger (module M5).
- **Tees.** A branch opening larger than about a quarter to a third of the run diameter must be fitted with **guide bars** (a barred tee), or a pig can turn into the branch and stick.
- **Reducers.** The mainline should keep a constant internal diameter. Dual-diameter pigs exist but limit inspection options.
- **Branch outlets** (weldolets, sweepolets). Used for small connections such as instrument tappings and drains.

### Special pipeline components

- **Monolithic isolation joints** electrically separate the cathodically protected pipeline from station piping, without the leak paths of an insulated flange.
- **Anchor flanges** are forged rings welded into the line and cast into a concrete anchor block to hold thermal forces (module M2).
- **Scraper trap closures** are quick-opening end closures with a safety interlock that prevents opening under pressure.

### Choosing a valve

| Duty | Usual choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mainline block valve, gas | Full-bore trunnion ball valve | Quarter turn, tight shut-off, piggable |
| Mainline block valve, liquids | Through-conduit gate or ball valve | Piggable, tolerant of solids |
| Scraper trap isolation | Full-bore ball or gate valve | Pig must pass |
| Backflow prevention | Swing or axial-flow check valve | Automatic |
| Flow or pressure control | Globe or axial control valve | Stable throttling |
| Small drains and vents | Ball or plug valve | Simple, compact |

Other features to specify:

- **Full bore or reduced bore.** Anything on the piggable route must be full bore.
- **End connections.** Welded ends for buried mainline valves; flanged where the valve must be removable.
- **Double block and bleed.** Two seats with a vent between them, to prove isolation before maintenance.
- **Actuator.** Manual gearbox, electric, hydraulic, or gas-over-oil using line gas. Remote and automatic operation is covered under sectionalizing valves in module M11.
- **Buried service.** Stem extension, external coating, and sealant, drain and vent lines brought above ground.
- **Fire-safe design and testing** for hydrocarbon service.

<Worked title="Flange class for a 96 bar gas pipeline">

**Given:** design pressure 96 bar, design temperature 60 °C, carbon steel flanges (group 1.1).

**Class 300** at 60 °C is about 49 bar. Not enough.

**Class 600** is 100.2 bar at 50 °C and 93.2 bar at 100 °C. Interpolating to 60 °C:

$$
100.2 - \frac{60 - 50}{100 - 50} \times (100.2 - 93.2) = 98.8 \text{ bar}
$$

98.8 bar is above 96 bar, so **Class 600** is suitable, with 3 % margin.

**Check the hydrotest.** A pipeline strength test at 1.25 × 96 = 120 bar is below the flange test limit of 1.5 × 102.1 = 153 bar, so the valves and flanges can stay in the test section.

**What if the design temperature were 100 °C?** The Class 600 rating falls to 93.2 bar, below the design pressure, and Class 900 would be required.

</Worked>

## Common mistakes

- Reading the class number as a pressure in psi. Class 600 is rated 1480 psi, not 600.
- Forgetting temperature derating of flanges.
- Fitting a reduced-bore valve or an unbarred tee on a piggable line.
- Using a standard-strength flange on high-grade pipe without checking the hub thickness transition.
- Testing against a closed valve at a pressure above its seat test rating.

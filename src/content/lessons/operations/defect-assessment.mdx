## Fitness for service

No pipeline is free of defects. The question is never "is there a defect?" but "is the pipe still fit for service with it, and for how long?" Answering that lets an operator repair what matters and leave alone what does not.

## Corrosion defects

A corroded area is a patch of thinner wall. It fails when the remaining ligament can no longer carry the hoop stress and bulges outward. Two dimensions matter:

- **Depth** $d$, relative to wall thickness $t$.
- **Axial length** $L$. A long defect behaves like a pipe with a thinner wall. A short one is supported by the full-thickness pipe around it and is much stronger.

Circumferential width matters far less, because hoop stress acts across the axial dimension.

### Modified B31G

The most widely used method estimates the failure pressure as:

$$
P_f = \frac{2 \, t}{D} \, \sigma_{flow} \left[ \frac{1 - 0.85 \, d/t}{1 - 0.85 \, (d/t) / M} \right]
$$

- $\sigma_{flow}$ = SMYS + 69 MPa (10 ksi), an estimate of the stress at which ductile steel fails.
- $M$ is the **Folias factor**, which accounts for bulging. It grows with $L^2 / (D t)$: the longer the defect, the larger $M$ and the lower the failure pressure.
- The factor 0.85 approximates the real, irregular defect profile.

The **safe operating pressure** is the failure pressure divided by a safety factor, commonly 1.39 (the inverse of a 0.72 design factor). The ratio of MAOP to safe pressure is called the **estimated repair factor (ERF)**: above 1.0, the defect needs action.

Defects deeper than **80 % of the wall** are repaired regardless of the calculation.

### Levels of assessment

| Method | Uses | Conservatism |
|---|---|---|
| Original B31G | Depth and length, parabolic shape | Most conservative |
| Modified B31G | Depth and length, 0.85 dL shape | Moderate |
| RSTRENG (effective area) | The measured river-bottom profile of the defect | Least conservative of the three |
| DNV-RP-F101 | Depth and length, with tensile strength and measurement uncertainty | For higher-toughness modern steels |
| Finite-element analysis | Full geometry | Case by case |

### Growth and re-inspection

A defect that passes today will be deeper next year. Comparing two inspection runs, or using a conservative assumed rate, gives a corrosion growth rate. That sets the date by which each defect must be repaired or re-inspected.

## Dents and mechanical damage

A **plain dent** is a smooth change in curvature with no loss of wall. Under static pressure it is usually benign, and codes accept depths up to about 6 % of the diameter, subject to strain limits. Its real danger is fatigue under pressure cycling.

Dents become serious when combined with something else:

- **Dent with a gouge or crack.** The most severe form of mechanical damage. Cold-worked, cracked material at the base of the gouge can fail at low stress or after a delay. Requires prompt repair.
- **Dent on a weld** (girth or seam).
- **Dent with corrosion.**
- **Dents on the top of the pipe**, which suggest third-party impact. Bottom-side dents are usually from rocks and are restrained by them.

## Cracks

Stress corrosion cracks, fatigue cracks and seam weld flaws are assessed by **fracture mechanics**, which needs the crack depth and length, the stresses and the steel's **toughness**. Methods are given in API 579 and BS 7910. Low-toughness vintage seams (old low-frequency ERW) need particular care.

## Responding to inspection results

In-line inspection reports are sorted by urgency:

- **Immediate:** predicted failure pressure near MAOP, metal loss over 80 %, dents with metal loss or cracks. Pressure is reduced until the feature is excavated.
- **Scheduled:** features that will reach the limit within a defined period.
- **Monitored:** to be compared at the next inspection.

Each excavation is also a **verification dig**: the defect is measured in the ditch and compared with the tool's report, which validates (or corrects) the whole inspection.

Before an excavation on a reported severe defect, reduce the pressure, typically to 80 % of the recent operating level. Removing the soil removes restraint, and the crew is standing next to the feature.

<Worked title="Assessing a reported corrosion feature">

**Given:** NPS 24 × 12.7 mm X65, MAOP 96 bar. ILI reports external metal loss 45 % deep and 250 mm long.

**Geometry term:** L² / (D t) = 250² / (610 × 12.7) = 8.07.

**Folias factor:** M = √(1 + 0.6275 × 8.07 − 0.003375 × 8.07²) = √5.84 = 2.42.

**Flow stress:** 450 + 69 = 519 MPa.

**Failure pressure:**

$$
P_f = \frac{2 \times 12.7}{610} \times 519 \times \frac{1 - 0.85 \times 0.45}{1 - 0.85 \times 0.45 / 2.42} = 21.6 \times \frac{0.6175}{0.842} = 15.8 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Safe pressure:** 15.8 / 1.39 = 11.4 MPa = **114 bar**.

**ERF:** 96 / 114 = 0.84. Acceptable at MAOP.

**Next step:** with a growth rate of, say, 0.3 mm per year, the depth passes 60 % in about six years. Schedule recoating and re-inspection well before then.

</Worked>

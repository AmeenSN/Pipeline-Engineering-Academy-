## Why detect leaks

The damage from a leak grows with time. A leak detection system exists to shorten the time between the start of a release and the response: confirm, shut down, isolate, dispatch.

Leaks range from full-bore ruptures, obvious within minutes from pressure and flow, to pinholes that lose a fraction of a percent of flow and can run for weeks. Different methods cover different parts of that range.

## Judging a leak detection system

API RP 1130 uses four measures:

- **Sensitivity:** the smallest leak it can detect, and how quickly.
- **Reliability:** how often it alarms correctly and how rarely it alarms falsely. A system that cries wolf gets ignored.
- **Accuracy:** how well it estimates leak size and location.
- **Robustness:** whether it keeps working when an instrument fails or the pipeline is in an unusual operating state.

Improving one usually costs another. Tightening the alarm threshold raises sensitivity and also false alarms.

## Choosing a combination

A typical modern liquid transmission line uses:

- **SCADA alarms** for ruptures,
- a **CPM system** (mass balance with a transient model or statistical method) for medium leaks,
- **pressure wave** detection for fast location,
- **fibre-optic sensing** or sensing cables at high-consequence sections,
- regular **patrols**.

Gas lines rely more on SCADA, pressure monitoring, line-break valves and patrols, because gas compressibility makes software methods less sensitive.

Design considerations:

- **Instrumentation.** A leak detection system is only as good as its meters. Custody-quality flow measurement at both ends pays for itself.
- **Segments.** More intermediate measurement points give better sensitivity and location.
- **Transients.** Batch changes, pump starts and slack-line flow on hills are when false alarms occur and real leaks hide.
- **Shut-in monitoring.** Leaks are easiest to detect when the line is shut in under pressure.
- **Testing.** Systems are proven by controlled fluid withdrawal tests.

## The human part

API RP 1175 treats leak detection as a **programme**, not a device: defined performance targets, alarm management, clear authority for the controller to shut down on a credible leak alarm without seeking permission, training with simulated leaks, and review of every alarm.

<Worked title="Locating a leak on a 50 km segment">

**Given:** segment length 50 km, wave speed 1100 m/s. The pressure drop is detected at the upstream sensor 16.4 s after it starts, and at the downstream sensor 29.1 s after. Only the difference is actually measurable: 12.7 s, upstream first.

$$
x = \frac{L + a \, (t_1 - t_2)}{2} = \frac{50\,000 + 1100 \times (-12.7)}{2} = 18\,015 \text{ m}
$$

The leak is about **18.0 km** from the upstream end.

**Uncertainty:** if each time stamp is good to 0.1 s, the position error is about 1100 × 0.1 / 2 = 55 m. With 1 s resolution it would be 550 m.

</Worked>

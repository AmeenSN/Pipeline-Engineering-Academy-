## What a pig is

A pig is a device that travels through the pipeline, pushed by the flow, sealing against the pipe wall. In Saudi Aramco and some other companies it is called a **scraper**, and the launcher and receiver are **scraper traps**.

Pigs are used throughout a pipeline's life:

- **Construction:** cleaning, gauging, filling for hydrotest, dewatering and drying.
- **Operation:** removing liquids, wax, scale and debris; applying inhibitor and biocide; separating product batches.
- **Integrity:** in-line inspection of the pipe wall.
- **Decommissioning:** cleaning and displacing product.

## Launchers and receivers

A trap is a short length of oversize pipe (the **barrel**) connected to the pipeline through a full-bore valve, with a quick-opening closure at the end. It lets a pig be inserted or removed while the pipeline stays in service.

Step through the sequence. Watch which valves change at each step and how flow is diverted through the kicker line at the moment of launch.

A **receiver** works in reverse. Its barrel is longer, to hold a long inspection tool plus debris. The bypass line leaves from the front of the barrel so flow continues after the pig has arrived, and the pig is recovered after the trap is isolated, depressurised and drained.

### The safety-critical step

Opening a trap closure that is still under pressure has killed operators. Defences, all used together:

- a **pressure-warning interlock** on the closure that cannot be released while pressure remains,
- a **pressure gauge and an open vent** on the barrel, checked before the closure is touched,
- **double isolation** with a bleed between the pipeline and the barrel,
- standing **to the side** of the door, never in front,
- for sour service, breathing apparatus and gas monitoring; for pyrophoric iron sulphide debris, keeping it wet.

## Designing a piggable line

Whether inspection tools can pass is decided at design stage:

- **Constant internal diameter.** Limit wall thickness changes; use transition pieces. Dual-diameter lines restrict tool choice.
- **Full-bore mainline valves.**
- **Bends of 3 D or, preferably, 5 D radius and larger** (module M5), with straight pipe between bends.
- **Barred tees** on any branch larger than about a quarter to a third of the line diameter.
- **No mitre bends, no internal obstructions,** no probes or thermowells protruding into the bore.
- **Check valves** of a piggable type.
- **Traps sized for inspection tools:** barrel length, working space in front of the closure, lifting facilities and drainage.
- **Pig signallers** downstream of the launcher and upstream of the receiver.
- **Flow conditions** within the tool's speed range, typically 0.5 to 4 m/s, which in gas lines may need the flow reduced during a run.

## Running pigs

- **Progressive pigging.** In a line that has not been pigged for years, start with soft foam pigs and work up through progressively more aggressive pigs, so that debris is removed a little at a time and a pig does not jam behind a wall of wax or scale.
- **Tracking.** Pigs carry transmitters, and crews follow them at above-ground marker points.
- **Stuck pigs** are located by the transmitter and by pressure readings, and freed by raising differential pressure, reversing flow, or as a last resort cutting out.
- **Frequency** is set by what comes out: the debris received is measured and analysed after every run.

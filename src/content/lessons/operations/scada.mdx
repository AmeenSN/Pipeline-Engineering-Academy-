## Operating at a distance

SCADA stands for **supervisory control and data acquisition**. It gathers measurements from every station and valve site along the pipeline, presents them to controllers in a central control room, and carries their commands back to the field. Without it, a long pipeline would need staff at every site around the clock.

SCADA **supervises**. Fast, safety-critical actions, such as tripping a pump on high pressure, are done locally by the station's own control and safety systems, which must work even if communication with the control room is lost.

## What the controller sees and does

**Monitored** at each site, typically:

- pressure and temperature upstream and downstream,
- flow rate and totals at metering points,
- valve positions and actuator status,
- pump or compressor status, speed and vibration,
- pig signaller status,
- cathodic protection rectifier output,
- power, battery, intrusion and fire alarms.

**Controlled** from the centre:

- starting and stopping pumps or compressors,
- pressure and flow set points,
- opening and closing mainline and station valves,
- initiating an emergency shutdown.

Controllers use this to keep the line inside its limits: below MAOP at every point, above minimum pressures needed to avoid slack-line flow or vapour formation, and within contracted delivery rates.

## Alarm management

An alarm should mean one thing: something abnormal that needs the controller to act. Systems that flood the controller with hundreds of alarms an hour train people to acknowledge without reading.

Good practice (API RP 1167):

- every alarm has a defined cause, consequence and required response,
- alarms are **prioritised** so the critical few stand out,
- **nuisance and standing alarms** are tracked and eliminated,
- alarm rates per controller are measured against targets,
- set points are documented and changes controlled.

## Control room management

Investigations of major pipeline incidents repeatedly find the same human factors: alarms ignored, shift handover gaps, fatigue, and controllers unsure whether they were allowed to shut down. API RP 1168 addresses these:

- clear **roles and authority**, including the authority to shut down on a suspected leak,
- structured **shift handover**,
- **fatigue management** through shift design,
- **training** with simulators, including abnormal and emergency scenarios,
- **management of change** for anything affecting the control room,
- learning from incidents and near misses.

## Cybersecurity

A SCADA system connected to corporate networks and the internet is a target. Defences follow the IEC 62443 approach:

- **Segmentation:** the control network is separated from business networks by firewalls and a demilitarised zone.
- **Access control:** individual accounts, strong authentication, least privilege.
- **Hardening and patching** of servers, workstations and field devices.
- **Monitoring** for intrusion and abnormal traffic.
- **Backups and a tested recovery plan,** including the ability to operate manually.

<Callout type="field">
When communication to a site is lost, the control room is blind there but the site is not out of control. Local logic keeps it in a safe state. Procedures define how long the line may run with a site unmonitored before someone is sent or the line is shut down.
</Callout>

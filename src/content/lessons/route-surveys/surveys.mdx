## Why survey

A desk study tells you roughly where the pipeline can go. Surveys tell you exactly what is there: the shape of the ground, what it is made of, what is buried in it and what crosses it. The results fix the centreline and feed almost every later calculation.

## From survey to profile

The topographic survey gives ground level at regular intervals along the centreline. Plotting level against distance gives the **ground profile**. The designer then draws the **pipe profile** below it:

1. Offset the ground line by the required cover to get a first pipe line.
2. Smooth it, because the pipe cannot follow every small bump. Where the ground curves more gently than the minimum elastic radius, the pipe follows it elastically.
3. Add field bends where the ground changes slope more sharply.
4. Deepen the pipe at crossings and wadis.
5. Check the result for cover, bend count and excavation quantity.

The finished profile, with the plan above it, becomes the alignment sheet (next lesson).

## Soil parameters the designer needs

| Parameter | Used for |
|---|---|
| Soil classification and density | Trench stability, backfill, soil loads |
| Friction angle and cohesion | Anchor resistance, uplift resistance, pipe-soil friction |
| Rock level and strength | Trenching method, padding, blasting |
| Groundwater level and salinity | Buoyancy, dewatering, corrosion |
| Resistivity | Cathodic protection design |
| Sulphate and chloride | Concrete and coating selection |
| Thermal conductivity | Heat loss, hot-line design |

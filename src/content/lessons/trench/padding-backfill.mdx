## Why the fill matters

Once the pipe is lowered in, the material placed around it has four jobs:

- **Protect the coating** from stones and impact.
- **Support the pipe** evenly underneath and at the sides, so it does not ovalise or sag.
- **Restrain the pipe** against uplift and movement.
- **Not corrode it:** the fill is the electrolyte in contact with any coating defect.

## Padding

Padding is the fine, stone-free material in contact with the pipe.

- **Extent:** all round the pipe and to about 150 to 300 mm above its crown.
- **Grading:** fine enough not to damage coating. Specifications typically limit the maximum particle to 10 to 20 mm, with rounded particles preferred and tighter limits for thin coatings such as FBE.
- **Under the haunches:** material must flow or be worked under the lower half of the pipe. Voids there leave the pipe sitting on a line contact.
- **Chemistry:** free of salts, organic matter and construction debris.

**Padding machines** travel along the trench, pick up spoil, screen it and discharge the fines over the pipe in one pass. They remove the need to import sand where the spoil contains enough fine material.

## Protecting coating in rock

| Method | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sand bedding and padding | Fine layer all round the pipe | Most reliable; may need imported material |
| Rock shield | Tough extruded plastic mesh wrapped round the pipe | Quick; open mesh lets cathodic protection current reach the pipe |
| Concrete or rock-jacket coating | Factory-applied armour over the corrosion coating | For severe rock and long distances |
| Foam pillows or sandbag benches | Supports at intervals keep the pipe off the trench bottom | Needs padding placed around them to avoid spans |
| Geotextile wrap | Felt layer around the pipe | Light protection |

Any wrap must not **shield** the pipe from cathodic protection. Solid plastic sheet around a pipe is a known cause of hidden corrosion.

## Backfilling

1. **Inspect first.** Coating holiday-tested as the pipe is lowered, trench clear of debris, pipe resting on the bottom.
2. **Backfill soon** after lowering-in, to protect the pipe and close the trench.
3. **Padding first,** placed gently. Never drop backfill from height directly onto the pipe.
4. **Then general backfill.** Keep large rocks out or place them last and away from the pipe.
5. **Warning tape** laid above the pipe as filling proceeds.
6. **Compaction** where specified: at roads, around tie-ins and bends on hot lines, and on slopes. Elsewhere the backfill is left with a **crown** to allow for natural settlement.
7. **Trench breakers** on slopes: barriers of sandbags or foam across the trench to stop water flowing along it.
8. **Leave tie-in points open** until welded, tested and coated.

<Callout type="field">
On a hot line, do not backfill in the cool of the night if you can avoid it. Backfilling when the pipe is warm locks it in at a higher temperature, which reduces compressive stress in service.
</Callout>

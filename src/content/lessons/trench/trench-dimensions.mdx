## What the trench does

Burial protects the pipeline from vehicles, tools, sun, fire and temperature swings, and the surrounding soil restrains it against pressure and thermal forces. The trench has to be deep enough to give that protection, wide enough to lower the pipe in without scraping its coating, and smooth enough to support it evenly.

## Depth

$$
\text{trench depth} = \text{cover} + \text{pipe diameter} + \text{bedding}
$$

**Cover** is measured from the top of the pipe (or of its concrete coating) to the finished ground surface. Code minimums:

| Location | ASME B31.4 | ASME B31.8 |
|---|---|---|
| Open country | 0.75 m | 0.61 m (Class 1) |
| Industrial, commercial, residential | 0.9 m | 0.76 m (Class 2 to 4) |
| Rock excavation | 0.45 to 0.6 m | 0.45 to 0.61 m |
| Road and rail ditches | 0.9 m | 0.9 m |
| River and stream crossings | 1.2 m | By assessment |

Most operators specify more than this: around 1 m in open country is common, with extra cover in farmland (below plough depth), at crossings, in wadis and in moving sand. Hot lines may need extra cover against upheaval buckling.

More cover is not always better. It raises excavation cost and soil load, and makes later repair slower.

## Width

The bottom must be wide enough for the pipe plus working clearance:

$$
\text{bottom width} \approx D + 2 \times \text{clearance}
$$

with a clearance of about 150 to 300 mm each side, more where workers must enter (tie-ins) and where padding must be compacted beside the pipe. A trench that is too narrow scrapes the coating during lowering-in; one that is too wide wastes excavation and backfill.

## Side slopes

The sides must stand until the pipe is in and backfilled:

- **Rock:** near-vertical.
- **Stiff clay and cemented soil:** steep, about 1 horizontal to 2 or 4 vertical.
- **Ordinary soil:** about 1 to 2.
- **Loose dry sand:** as flat as 1.5 horizontal to 1 vertical.
- **Wet or saturated ground:** flat slopes plus dewatering or sheet piling.

## The trench bottom

The pipe should rest on the trench bottom along its full length.

- **Graded and smooth,** following the designed profile, with no humps, hollows or hard points.
- **Free of rocks, roots, skids and debris.** Welding rod stubs and timber left under the pipe damage coating.
- **Bedded** with fine material where the natural bottom is rock or gravel.
- **Dry** enough to see and inspect. A flooded trench hides everything.

Local high spots matter twice: they dent the pipe, and on a hot line each one is an imperfection that can start upheaval buckling.

## Trenching methods

- **Hydraulic excavators** are the general-purpose tool, used almost everywhere.
- **Wheel and chain trenchers** cut a neat, uniform trench quickly in suitable soil and soft rock.
- **Rock breakers, rippers and blasting** for hard rock.
- **Ploughs** for small-diameter lines in open, soft country.

Trenching is usually kept a controlled distance ahead of welding and lowering-in. Too far, and the open trench collapses, fills with windblown sand or floods, and is a hazard to people and animals. Crossings for livestock, wildlife and local traffic are left at intervals.

## Safety in trenches

Trench collapse kills quickly and without warning. A cubic metre of soil weighs well over a tonne.

- No one enters a trench deeper than about 1.2 to 1.5 m unless it is sloped, benched, shored or shielded.
- Spoil and equipment are kept back from the edge (at least 0.6 m, more in weak ground).
- Ladders or ramps for exit at short intervals.
- Daily inspection by a competent person, and after rain.
- Gas testing where hydrocarbons, H₂S or oxygen deficiency are possible.
- Barriers and lights where the public or traffic are near.

**Given:** OD 0.61 m, cover 1.0 m, no bedding, 0.3 m clearance each side, side slope 0.5 horizontal to 1 vertical.

**Depth:** 1.0 + 0.61 = 1.61 m.

**Bottom width:** 0.61 + 2 × 0.3 = 1.21 m.

**Top width:** 1.21 + 2 × 0.5 × 1.61 = 2.82 m.

**Excavation per metre:** (1.21 + 2.82) / 2 × 1.61 = **3.24 m³/m**, or 3240 m³ per kilometre.

**In loose sand** (slope 1.5 to 1) the top width becomes 6.04 m and the volume 5.84 m³/m: 80 % more digging for the same pipe.
